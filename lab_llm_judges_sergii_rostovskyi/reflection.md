# Reflection

## 1. What would change if the client's data was in French?

I would build the evaluation from original French tickets and the French storefront's policy. Translating an English benchmark would preserve some useful boundary cases, but the phrasing customers use for delays, damaged goods, and complaints could change. I would also cover the French locales the client actually serves and have the client confirm which policies are shared across those markets.

I would retain exact checks for currency and outcomes while making sure equivalent formats such as “48 €” and “48,00 €” are accepted when amounts appear in prose. Two native French-speaking reviewers would label correctness, clarity, and tone, including the use of “vous” and the handling of frustrated customers. Their labels would be used to calibrate the judge on French paraphrases and ambiguous cases before judging the holdout.

## 2. How would I answer a client asking whether this model is AGI-level?

I would ask what observable capability the client wants the term to represent. If the concern is whether the model can manage an entire support operation, I would translate that into measurable tasks such as handling exceptions, following policy updates, and escalating cases appropriately. Five drafting tasks only provide evidence about a small portion of that workflow.

A broader claim would need a stated definition, private tasks across unfamiliar domains, repeated trials, and clear comparisons with competent humans working under similar conditions. It would also need tests of how the system behaves when the information is incomplete or contradictory. Any conclusion would be limited to the tasks, access, and evaluation conditions actually tested.

## 3. What is one thing I could not evaluate without a human?

I could not confidently judge whether a reply to a repeated complaint would restore the customer's trust. A rule can check that the reply mentions previous contact, and a judge can flag generic language. Neither can tell whether someone who has already tried twice would find the response sincere or believe the proposed next step.

I would ask experienced support staff to score anonymized replies independently and explain which wording would help move the case forward. If the client approves a small supervised trial, I would compare those judgments with customer feedback and repeat contacts. I would review disagreements before using empathy scores as a release signal.
