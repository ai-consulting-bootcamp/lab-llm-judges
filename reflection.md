# Step 6 — Evaluation Reflection

## Question 1 — What would change if the client's data were in French?

If MNM AG's vendor-management documents were in French, the evaluation would need French-language test questions, reference answers and company policies. English-language benchmarks alone would not adequately assess the assistant's ability to understand French financial terminology or provide accurate procedural guidance.

The evaluation should include native or professionally proficient French speakers to verify translations, terminology and answer quality. Special attention would be needed for ambiguous terms, regional language differences and cases where the assistant incorrectly translates payment or procurement instructions. The LLM judge would also need calibration using French reference examples to ensure that language style does not unfairly influence scores.

## Question 2 — How would you respond if the client asked whether the model is AGI-level?

I would explain that AGI, or Artificial General Intelligence, generally refers to AI capable of performing a broad range of intellectual tasks at a level comparable to humans, although there is no universally accepted definition or single test.

Our evaluation cannot establish whether a model is AGI-level. It only measures performance on five specific vendor-management tasks. Even perfect results would demonstrate competence within this limited scope, not general intelligence.

A broader assessment would require independent evaluations across reasoning, planning, unfamiliar tasks, adaptability, reliability and multiple domains. Any conclusions would need to acknowledge the limitations of current benchmarks and the difficulty of defining and measuring AGI.

## Question 3 — What is one thing you could not reliably evaluate without a human, and why?

One important aspect requiring human evaluation is whether the assistant's guidance is appropriate for MNM AG's actual business processes and operational risks.

An LLM judge can compare an answer with predefined policies, but it cannot independently confirm whether those policies are current, correctly interpreted or suitable for a real business situation. For example, a bank-detail change may require additional verification because of suspected fraud or unusual supplier behaviour.

A qualified procurement or finance employee should therefore review selected responses, especially high-risk or ambiguous cases. Human reviewers can assess business context, identify missing safeguards and provide feedback to improve the evaluation criteria. Automated scoring should support, rather than replace, this judgment.
