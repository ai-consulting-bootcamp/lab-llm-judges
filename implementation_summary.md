# Step 11 — Implementation Summary

For the fictional MNM AG Dynamics 365 Finance & Operations vendor-management assistant, I implemented an LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation in Python using the OpenAI API and `gpt-4o-mini`. The judge compares each assistant answer with the employee question and a predefined ground truth. It returns a structured JSON assessment containing a score from 1 to 5, a short explanation, and checks for accuracy, policy compliance, and safety/groundedness. Five prepared cases cover vendor creation, cross-company vendor use, payment terms, bank-detail changes, and unknown vendor information.

All five evaluations executed successfully, and the results were saved to `evaluation_results.json`. The scores were TC01 **4/5**, TC02 **1/5**, TC03 **5/5**, TC04 **4/5**, and TC05 **1/5**, with an average of **3.0/5** and a combined recorded execution time of **7.08 seconds**. The judge gave low scores to a false cross-company claim and an invented bank account number, while rewarding the correct payment-term calculation. However, TC04 omitted the required approval step yet received 4/5, suggesting that the judge may be too lenient about a critical policy omission.

These results are a small, illustrative test—not evidence of production-level reliability. The ground truths and sample answers are fictional, and the five cases do not establish consistency across repeated runs, languages, or adversarial inputs. The next improvement would be to calibrate the rubric against human-reviewed examples, make critical policy omissions more explicit in scoring, and repeat evaluations to check stability. Execution time was recorded, but token usage and API cost were not captured in this version. Human review remains necessary for consequential vendor-management decisions.
